Food is the difference between efficient mining and exhausted wandering. Without it, you can't sprint, can't regenerate health, and on Hard difficulty, you literally starve.

But here's the mistake most new players make: they treat all food as equal. They'll eat rotten flesh (80% chance of poison) instead of walking 30 seconds to find a cow. They'll farm wheat but never understand that saturation matters more than visible hunger restoration.

Saturation is the hidden metric. Your hunger bar shows 20 points, but there's an invisible saturation bar that drains first. A food that restores 8 hunger but adds 12.8 saturation will keep you fed for much longer than a food that restores 8 hunger but adds only 2 saturation.

## The Mechanics

Your hunger system works in layers:

**Saturation drains first.** When you do work (mining, sprinting, jumping), your saturation decreases. Only when saturation reaches zero does visible hunger start draining. This is why high-saturation foods feel like they keep you full longer—they literally do.

**At 18 hunger (9 shanks full):** You can sprint indefinitely.

**Below 18 hunger:** No more sprinting. Walking only.

**Below 6 hunger:** Health stops regenerating even if you're not taking damage.

**At 0 hunger:** On Hard, you start taking starvation damage. On Normal, you just can't regenerate. On Easy, nothing bad happens.

## Tier 1: Day 1-3 (Survival Mode)

On your first day, you need *something* fast. Don't hunt for optimal foods—hunt for *immediate* foods.

**Cooked Chicken** — Punch a chicken, wait for it to drop on an accidental fire (or place one quickly with flint + steel), and you have food immediately. It's not optimal long-term, but it gets you through night one.

**Raw Apples** — Break oak/dark oak logs, and 1-2% chance they drop raw apples. Eat immediately, no cooking needed. Unreliable but free.

**Bread** — Once you've found wheat or tall grass, break grass for seeds. Plant them in tilled dirt (hoe + dirt), wait 10 minutes, harvest. Craft 3 wheat into bread. Medium saturation. Boring but works.

The worst mistake here is eating **raw chicken** without cooking. Yes, it restores hunger, but 30% chance of poisoning means you'll waste healing items. Always cook it first.

## Tier 2: Days 4-15 (Establishing Farms)

Now you've got time to build. The food landscape changes because you're thinking long-term.

**Cooked Steak/Porkchop** — Find a cow or pig, breed them with wheat (cows) or carrots (pigs). Build a small pen. Cook the drops. This is the benchmark good food source. 8 hunger restored, 12.8 saturation. It carries you through the entire game.

**Golden Carrots** — If you've found gold nuggets or have killed enough zombies for carrots, craft these. 6 hunger, 14.4 saturation. They're the single best saturation food you can make. One golden carrot with some bread keeps you full longer than four cooked steaks.

**Baked Potatoes** — Potatoes drop from zombies or grow in villages. They're easy but slightly lower saturation than steak.

**Cooked Fish** — Fishing is passive income. Catch fish, cook them, use as backup food when animal farms aren't producing.

## Tier 3: Mid-Late Game (Optimization)

At this point you have options. Choose based on what you're doing.

**Mining marathon?** Bring cooked steak and water. The saturation means you won't need to eat constantly.

**PvP or raiding?** Bring golden carrots. High saturation lets you go longer between bites, meaning more time fighting.

**Exploring?** Bread is light and travels well. Or golden carrots for efficiency.

## Setup Priority

**Month 1 priority:** Cow or pig farm. Just the basics. Two animals, a breeding pen, a furnace nearby. Cook everything.

**Month 2:** Add a potato farm next to it. Doubles your food without much effort.

**Month 3 (if you have gold):** Golden carrots. This is a quality-of-life upgrade, not a necessity.

Don't overthink it early. Cooked meat sustains you. Everything else is optimization.

## Foods That Actively Waste Your Time

Avoid these entirely. They're worse than nothing.

**Raw Chicken (30% poison), Rotten Flesh (80% poison), Spider Eye (100% poison):** Eating these means antidotes or waiting out poison effects. Not worth it when better foods exist.

**Pufferfish:** Causes poisoning + hunger + nausea. Never eat this. It exists for potions.

**Cake:** Technically food (1.5 hunger per slice), but it takes 3 planks + egg + milk + sugar to craft. The return is terrible compared to a cow farm.

## Final Thoughts

You don't need a complicated food strategy to survive Minecraft. Get a cow or pig farm running as soon as possible, keep a furnace handy for cooking, and never venture far from home on an empty stomach.

Golden carrots are worth investing in once you have a gold source. The saturation boost is dramatic for long dungeon runs or mining sessions where you can't easily eat.
