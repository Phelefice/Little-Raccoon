Villager trading is the economic backbone of late-game survival. It's the difference between spending hours hunting enchanted books in dungeons and buying them for emeralds. It's the difference between mining diamonds and farming emeralds to buy diamond tools.

Understanding villagers isn't optional if you want an efficient base. It's foundational.

## Professions and Job Site Blocks

Unemployed villagers (plain brown robes, no badge) take professions when you place the corresponding job site block near them. The block must be accessible—same ground level, no obstacles.

The professions you actually care about:

**Librarian (Lectern)** — Sells enchantment books. This is the most important profession. Mending, Silk Touch, Fortune, Sharpness, Protection, Unbreaking—they all come from librarians. Curing a librarian drops prices from 20+ emeralds to 1.

**Farmer (Composter)** — Buys crops (wheat, pumpkins, melons, potatoes, carrots, beetroot) and sells emeralds. A single farmer with a crop farm feeding into it can generate hundreds of emeralds per day. This is your emerald factory.

**Armorer (Blast Furnace)** — Sells enchanted diamond armor pieces. After maxing out their trade tier, they offer protection and other enchantments already applied.

**Cleric (Brewing Stand)** — Sells Ender Pearls, Redstone, Glowstone, Bottles o' Enchanting. The Ender Pearls alone make this worth having.

**Cartographer (Cartography Table)** — Sells maps to ocean monuments and woodland mansions. Once you have one, you don't need more—you can copy the maps yourself with cartographers.

**Weaponsmith (Grindstone)** — Sells enchanted iron swords and axes. Less useful after mid-game since you'll have diamonds, but good early on.

**Toolsmith (Smithing Table)** — Sells enchanted diamond pickaxes, axes, shovels. Similar to weaponsmith—useful early, less so late.

There are others (fisherman, shepherd, etc.) but these are the core ones.

## Assigning Professions (The Simple Part)

Place a job site block within a few blocks of an unemployed villager. They'll walk toward it and claim the profession after a moment. The profession locks in once they've had access to the block for a few seconds.

Two important points:

**Beds matter:** Villagers need assigned beds to maintain professions and breed. Give each villager a bed within range or they'll abandon trades.

**Before you trade = free rerolls:** If you haven't traded with a villager yet, you can break their job site block and replace it (or break it and re-place it later) to reroll their offers. Once you buy anything, their trades lock permanently.

## Curing Zombie Villagers (The Key to Wealth)

This is the most important mechanic in the trading system. Cured villagers offer permanent discounts on all trades—often 50-90% off their normal prices.

The process:

1. Find a zombie villager (looks like a zombie but wears villager robes)
2. Trap it in a dark area or give it a roof (so it won't burn during day)
3. Splash Potion of Weakness on it (brewed with fermented spider eye)
4. Feed it a Golden Apple (crafted from 8 gold blocks + 1 apple center)
5. Wait 3-5 minutes. Shaking = converting. Green particles = done.

After curing, that villager offers a "hero of the village" discount. A Librarian that costs 20 emeralds per Mending book? Now costs 2-3 emeralds. This stacks multiplicatively with bulk trades.

Curing villagers is so powerful that establishing a zombie village curing station is endgame-mandatory if you want efficiency.

## Building a Trading Hub

A viable late-game setup includes:

- **1 Librarian** (cure 2-3 times for discount). Lock in Mending immediately.
- **1-2 Farmers** (connected to crop farm)
- **1 Cleric** (for Ender Pearls and Redstone)
- **1 Armorer** (for diamond gear)
- **1 Cartographer** (copy their maps)

Give each a bed, keep them contained so they don't wander into danger. This setup generates unlimited Mending books, unlimited diamond tools, unlimited emeralds from crops.

## Common Mistakes

**Mistake:** Not curing villagers. You lose massive discounts.

**Mistake:** Breaking a job block after trading, expecting to reroll. Once traded, they're locked.

**Mistake:** Not giving villagers beds. They'll abandon their jobs.

**Mistake:** Trading with a librarian before getting Mending. Reroll first, lock in Mending, THEN trade.

The last one costs more time than anything else because librarians have randomized book offers and Mending is rare.

Villagers need three things: a bed, a job site, and access to trading.

To keep them in place:

- Build enclosed trading cells with one bed and one job site per villager.
- Use trapdoors or fences to block pathfinding while still allowing trades.
- Never leave large open spaces where villagers can roam and lose their beds.

A proper trading hall locks each villager in a 1x2 cell. They sleep, work, and trade without ever leaving.

## Breeding New Villagers

Villagers breed when they have enough food and unclaimed beds nearby. Throw bread, carrots, potatoes, or beetroot into a villager's inventory and place new beds in the area. Two willing villagers will produce a baby.

Use this mechanic to populate your trading hall without relying on finding new villages.

## Final Thoughts

Villagers transform survival Minecraft from a grind into a system. Once you have a cured librarian with Mending, a farmer generating emeralds, and an armorer supplying gear, your survival world becomes almost limitless in its potential.

Build your trading hall early. Cure every villager you can. Unlock Mending first. Everything else will follow naturally.
