The spear fundamentally changed how melee combat works in Minecraft. For years, swords were the only choice. Strong, reliable, boring. The spear—introduced in the Mounts of Mayhem update (1.21.11)—adds a skill expression element that swords simply don't have.

Unlike swords, spear damage scales with momentum. Attack while standing still and you deal baseline damage. Sprint forward and charge your attack, and you hit significantly harder. Ride a horse at full gallop into an enemy? The damage becomes devastating. This one mechanic makes mounted combat actually worth engaging with, which is why the spear feels like it should have existed from the beginning.

## Crafting and Material Tiers

The spear follows the standard tool crafting pattern. You need two sticks and one material in the top slot of a crafting grid—think of it as the slanted cousin of a shovel recipe.

Each material tier has different stats:

- **Wood/Stone/Iron/Gold/Diamond:** Progressively higher base damage and durability
- **Netherite:** Highest tier, requires upgrading a diamond spear in a smithing table

You don't *need* to craft one. Spears spawn as loot in Buried Treasure, Bastion Remnants, and End Cities. Mobs like Zombies, Husks, Piglins, and Zombified Piglins can also drop them. But early on, crafting a wooden or stone spear is faster than mining.

In survival, aim for an iron spear by mid-game. It has enough durability to survive combat and enough damage to matter.

## Two Completely Different Attacks

This is where the spear gets interesting—and where most players mess up initially.

**Jab Attack** — Press attack button without holding. Fires off quickly with extra reach compared to swords (but only at medium range). Hits multiple targets and knocks them back. Great for crowd control or mobile combat where you're not charging.

**Charge Attack** — Hold attack button to wind up. Damage increases based on how fast you're moving when you release. After about 5 seconds, the charge resets, and you enter different states (Engaged → Tired → Disengaged). The timer resets when you land a hit.

Most new players spam jabs because they feel similar to sword combat. Veterans understand that charge attacks are where the power lies. The difference is massive.

## Damage Scaling: Movement Matters

Here's the unique part: your velocity when you attack determines your damage output.

Standing still and charging → ~8 damage
Sprinting forward and charging → ~15 damage
Mounted at full gallop and charging → ~25-30 damage

This is why the spear becomes genuinely overpowered on horseback. You can dismount enemies from distance. You can kite mob groups. Mounted PvP becomes a skill-based sport instead of both players standing still poking each other.

## Lunge Enchantment (Spear Exclusive)

Lunge is unique to spears and works only with jab attacks. It propels you horizontally in the direction you're facing—basically a built-in dash.

- Level I: 1 hunger
- Level II: 2 hunger  
- Level III: 3 hunger

Lunge doesn't work if you're below 6 hunger, riding, gliding, or underwater. It's useful for kiting, positioning, or escaping fights. On high-hunger builds (like ones with good food), Lunge III lets you spam short bursts of mobility.

## Full Enchantment Support

Spears play nice with standard enchantments:

- **Sharpness/Smite:** Damage boosts. Note: tables cap at Sharpness IV. Combine two Sharpness IV spears in an anvil for V.
- **Unbreaking/Mending:** Durability
- **Looting:** Drops from mobs (useful if farming with spears)
- **Knockback:** Stacks with the jab's innate knockback for mob control

Sharpness is the obvious pick for most builds because the damage multiplier compounds with charge scaling.

## Early Game Vs Late Game

Early: Craft a stone spear, use it for basic combat and farming mobs. Jabs work fine.

Mid: Upgrade to iron, add Sharpness IV if you can. Charge attacks start to feel powerful.

Late: Netherite spear with Sharpness V, Unbreaking III, Mending. Pair with horse and full leather armor (for mobility) or diamond (for safety). Charge attacks one-shot most mobs.

## Mounted Spear Combat (The Real Game)

Once you have a decent horse and an iron+ spear, mounted combat becomes viable. Gallop toward a creeper and charge attack—one hit kills it. A raid becomes manageable when you can loop through pillagers on horseback, charging between passes.

This is probably why spears exist as a weapon—they force you to engage with mounts differently. Before, horses were transportation. Now they're a viable combat platform.

## When to Use a Spear

Spears excel when you can move. Open terrain, riding horses, kiting mobs. They're less useful in confined spaces or against enemies you need to pin down (because charge attacks need space and momentum).

Pick a sword if you're cave diving and need fast, consistent damage. Pick a spear if you're on a horse, in open terrain, or playing PvP where mobility matters.
